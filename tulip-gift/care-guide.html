Quick reference
Month-by-Month at a Glance
Month What to Do Status
Oct – Nov Plant bulbs 6–8" deep, pointed-side up. Water thoroughly after planting. Plant
Dec – Feb Nothing to do! Bulbs are sleeping underground and building up energy. Rest
Mar – Apr Green shoots appear, then flowers open. Water at the base only if it hasn't rained. Bloom
Apr – May Snip off dead flower heads. Leave all the leaves and stems — do not cut them. Deadhead
May – June Once leaves are completely yellow and lying flat, carefully dig up the bulbs. Lift
June – Sept Let bulbs dry out for a few days, then keep in a cool dry place until October. Store

Common questions
Troubleshooting & Tips

My tulips came up but didn't flower — why?

+

The most common reason is cutting the leaves back too early the year before. The leaves feed energy back into the bulb after blooming — if you cut them while they're still green, the bulb doesn't get the fuel it needs to flower again. Another common cause is not enough cold weather over winter. Tulips actually need a long cold period (at least 12–14 weeks below 50°F) to trigger blooming — it's how they know winter is over and spring has arrived.

Do I have to dig up the bulbs every year?

+

If you live somewhere with hot, humid summers (think the American South), yes — the heat and moisture will rot the bulbs in the ground. If you're in a cooler climate with distinct seasons, you can sometimes leave them in the ground, but digging them up and storing them over summer almost always gives you better, bigger blooms the next spring. Think of it like giving the bulb a proper rest in good conditions rather than leaving it to survive whatever the summer throws at it.

Squirrels or chipmunks keep digging up my bulbs!

+

This is one of the most common frustrations for tulip growers! A few things that work well: lay a piece of chicken wire (a metal mesh you can find at any hardware store) flat over the soil right after planting and remove it in spring before shoots appear. You can also sprinkle crushed red pepper flakes into the planting hole — animals hate the smell. Planting a bit deeper than usual (8 inches instead of 6) also helps, since it's harder for them to find.

Can I cut them and put them in a vase?

+

Yes, and they make gorgeous cut flowers! The trick is to cut them while the bud is still closed but fully colored — not after they've already opened. Use sharp scissors and cut the stem at an angle (this helps the stem take up more water). Place them in a vase with cool, fresh water and keep them somewhere cool and away from direct sunlight. Tulips actually prefer cool temperatures and will last 7–10 days indoors. Change the water every couple of days to keep them fresh longer.
